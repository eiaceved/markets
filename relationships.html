Relationships

the map · what moves what

When one number moves, others follow. This is the plain-English map of what drags what, so the tape stops being eight separate numbers and starts being a few stories you can read at a glance.

The moves that travel

Five drivers that reach into everything else. If the driver is up or down, the arrows show what usually follows, and the small line says why. "Usually" is doing real work here: these are tendencies, not laws.

Oilup = pressure
  • ↑Gas & inflationfuel feeds into the price of nearly everything moved or made
  • ↑Energy stocksExxon, Chevron and friends earn more per barrel
  • ↓Airlines & shippingfuel is their biggest cost
  • ↓Odds of rate cutsthe Fed fights inflation, so higher oil pushes cuts further out
10-year yieldup = see-saw
  • ↓Bond pricesthe see-saw rule: yield up, existing bonds worth less. Always
  • ↑Mortgage rateshome loans price off the 10-year
  • ↓Tech & growth stockstheir profits sit far in the future, and higher yields shrink what that future is worth today
  • ↑The dollarhigher US yields pull foreign money toward US bonds
VIXthe fear gauge
  • ↓Stocks, usuallyVIX measures fear, and fear climbs when stocks drop. It reads the market more than it moves it
  • ↑Safe havensbonds, gold and the dollar catch the money leaving risky trades
  • ↑Insurance costsVIX is literally the price of protecting a stock portfolio
Dollarup = squeeze
  • ↓S&P multinationalsApple, Nike and friends earn abroad; those profits buy fewer dollars back home
  • ↓Gold & oilboth are priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar makes them pricier for everyone else
  • ↑Your importsforeign goods get cheaper for Americans
Goldup = a question
  • ↑Fear bidif stocks are falling with it, gold is the crisis hedge working
  • ↑Rate-cut betif yields are falling with it, traders expect easier money
  • ↑Inflation hedgeif inflation is climbing with it, it is buying power insurance
Read them togethergold's trick
  • ↑Gold alone says littlethe neighbours tell you which story is running: fear, rate cuts, or inflation. Same rule for every number: never read one line alone

The big three read together

The Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq are not three markets. They are three lenses on the same one, and the gap between them is the story.

Nasdaq up most, VIX lowA tech-led rally. Growth names are running and nobody is scared.
Dow up mostA defensive day: money rotating into big, boring, dividend-paying companies.
All three up togetherA broad rally, not a tech-only one. The strongest kind.
All three down togetherSomething macro is moving: rates, oil, or a shock somewhere.

Three scenes you will actually see

Real mornings come in patterns. Here are the three you will spot first.

The inflation scare
Oil ↑Yields ↑Stocks ↓Gold ↑

Costs are rising, the Fed looks less likely to cut, and the expensive tech names feel it hardest. You will know it by oil and yields climbing together.

The tech-led calm
S&P ↑Nasdaq ↑↑Yields flatVIX low

The growth trade is on. Nasdaq outruns the S&P, the Dow lags, and nothing looks frightened anywhere.

The flight to safety
Stocks ↓VIX ↑Yields ↓Gold ↑Dollar ↑

Worry about a war, a bank, or an election, and money hides in the usual places: bonds, gold, dollars. Falling yields with falling stocks is the tell.

The one-line versions

Yield up → bond prices downThe one true "always" in the strip.
Oil up → rate cuts pushed outInflation pressure makes the Fed's job harder.
VIX high = nervous marketIt is the price of insurance, not a prediction.
Dollar up → S&P earnings squeezedOverseas profits are worth less back home.
Never read one line aloneEvery number borrows its meaning from what moved with it.
